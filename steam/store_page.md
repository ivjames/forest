## Name

Lost in the Forest '88

## Short description (≤300 chars)

A DOS-era survival text adventure on a green-phosphor CRT. You wake lost in
the pines with nothing. Ten by ten cells of forest, a ranger station somewhere
in it, and a bear that hunts by smell. Type commands. Manage thirst, cold, and
night. Get out — or don't.

## About This Game (long description)

**It's 1988. The screen hums. You are lost.**

*Lost in the Forest '88* is a survival text adventure played the way they used to
be — by typing — rendered on a period-correct 4:3 EGA monitor with an emulated
1-bit PC-speaker beeper. No mouse. No hand-holding. A 10×10 wilderness, an `@`
that is you, and a ranger station that means rescue.

**Survive the systems, not a story:**

- **Water is the clock.** Thirst falls every turn. Find streams, carry a
  canteen, drink the rain.
- **Night is the wall.** Eight turns of daylight, then cold that kills.
  Fire, cave, or blanket — pick one before dusk.
- **The bear hunts by smell.** Wider range at night, narrower in rain. Tracks
  and a growl are your only warning. Fire turns it. The whistle scares it.
  The knife is a last resort.
- **Weather changes the rules.** Fog shortens sight. Rain douses fires.
  Storms forbid climbing.
- **Fifteen pieces of gear** buried in the trees — map, compass, flare, rope,
  lantern, fishing line — each one changes how you play.

**Three difficulties**, from forgiving Day Hike to brutal Survivalist. Climb
trees for bearings, signal with smoke, forage berry patches, fish the streams.
Score rewards speed, gear, and ground covered. Every forest is generated
fresh; every death is your fault.

Authentic DOS letterforms (the real Px437 IBM VGA font), a switchable
true-EGA 16-color hardware palette, scanlines, phosphor glow — and every
color meets WCAG AA contrast, with reduced-motion support.

**Type `help`. Then type `north`. Good luck.**
