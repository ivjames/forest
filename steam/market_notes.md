# Market notes — comparable games on Steam

Researched 2026-08-19. The comp set: deliberately-vintage aesthetics,
typed/text interfaces, and short-but-replayable survival runs.

## The comps

| Game | Price | Reviews | Rating | Why it's a comp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organ Trail: Director's Cut | $4.99 | ~2,400 | 89% Very Positive | The closest product shape: a vintage-clone survival game built on short, repeatable runs at an impulse price. |
| Stories Untold | $9.99 | ~4,300 | 86% Very Positive | Typing commands into period-correct hardware as the core fantasy. ~3 hours long, low replay — proof short is fine when the experience is distinct. |

## What the pattern says

1. **Price: $4.99.** That's the Organ Trail shelf — vintage-clone survival
   runs at impulse price. $2.99 signals shovelware more than it drives
   volume; above $10 the audience expects NEO-Scavenger-scale depth. Launch
   with the standard 10–15% opening discount.
2. **Sell the run, not the runtime.** Every successful comp is honest that a
   run is short (20–40 min) and the *game* is the repetition: new forest,
   harder difficulty, better score. Players punish "short" only when the
   page implied "long." Put "every forest is generated fresh" and the three
   difficulties front and center (store copy already does).
3. **Distinct-aesthetic-as-hook is a proven lane, not a risk.** Stories
   Untold and WORLD OF HORROR both charge real money for deliberately
   obsolete presentation. What separates them from the Mixed-review pile is
   *specificity* — real Px437 letterforms, a true-EGA palette, and a beeper
   are specific; "retro vibes" is not. The page should brag about the
   period-correctness in exact terms.
4. **This genre's review-killers**: deaths that feel like RNG theft (the
   bear needs to feel *trackable* — tracks/growl warnings are the defense,
   and worth a line on the store page), typing friction complaints from
   controller-first players (be upfront: keyboard game), and thin-content
   complaints when priced above the depth (stay at $4.99).
5. **Realistic expectations.** The successful comps had press moments or
   years of tail. A first release with no marketing typically lands 10–50
   reviews; the levers that move that are the free web version funneling
   wishlists, Steam Next Fest with the web game as demo, and achievements
   (this audience skews completionist — the v1.1 achievement list in
   STEAM.md is worth doing).
