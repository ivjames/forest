IT'S 1988. THE SCREEN HUMS. YOU ARE LOST.

A survival text adventure played the way they used to be — by typing — on a period-correct 4:3 EGA monitor with a 1-bit PC-speaker beeper. A 10×10 wilderness, an @ that is you, and a ranger station that means rescue.

SURVIVE THE SYSTEMS, NOT A STORY

  • Water is the clock. Thirst falls every turn. Streams, canteen, rain.
  • Night is the wall. Eight turns of daylight, then killing cold. Fire, cave, or blanket.
  • The bear hunts by smell. Tracks and a growl are your only warning.
  • Weather changes the rules. Fog shortens sight. Rain douses fires. Storms forbid climbing.
  • Fourteen pieces of gear buried in the trees, each one a new option.
  • Three difficulties. Day Hike is forgiving. Survivalist is not.

AUTHENTIC TO THE YEAR

The real Px437 IBM VGA letterforms. A switchable true-EGA 16-color hardware palette. Scanlines, phosphor, and an emulated beeper. Every forest generated fresh; every death your fault.

Type help. Then type north. Good luck.
